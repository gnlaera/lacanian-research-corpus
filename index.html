Lacanian Research Corpus

Primary Lacan, Freud, major interpreters, clinical sources and cross-source synthesis

A structured research corpus for the study and applied use of Lacanian psychoanalysis. The corpus reconstructs major concepts across Lacan's seminars and writings, connects them with Freud, distinguishes later interpretation from primary Lacan, and provides clinical and cross-source material for research, interpretation and reflective use.

Canonical corpus files

These ten files form the main structured Lacanian research corpus.

  1. M01 — Reference, Chronology, Terms and Écrits Core reference file for chronology, terminology, conceptual development, major writings and orientation to Lacan's Écrits and seminars.
  2. M02 — Lacan Seminars IV to VI Early mature Lacan: object relations, formations of the unconscious, desire, fantasy, signification and related developments.
  3. M03 — Lacan Seminar VII: The Ethics of Psychoanalysis Desire, ethics, das Ding, the pleasure principle, jouissance, sublimation and the ethical position of psychoanalysis.
  4. M04 — Lacan Seminars VIII to IX Transference, love, agalma, identification, the subject, the Other and the continuing development of Lacan's account of desire.
  5. M05 — Lacan Seminars X to XI Anxiety, object a, desire, drive, repetition, transference, the unconscious, alienation and separation.
  6. M06 — Late Lacan: Seminars XVI, XVII, XX and XXIII Discourse, jouissance, sexual difference, the non-relation, lalangue, symptom and sinthome in Lacan's later teaching.
  7. M07 — Major Interpreters Major later interpreters of Lacan, with attention to where later interpretation extends, reorganises or differs from primary Lacan.
  8. M08 — Companions and Clinical Perspectives Companion literature and clinically oriented explanations of Lacanian theory, technique and psychoanalytic practice.
  9. M09 — Freud Foundations Freudian foundations required for Lacanian study, including the unconscious, repetition, sexuality, narcissism, object choice, transference, drive and related concepts.
  10. M10 — Cross-Source Synthesis Integrated synthesis across Lacan, Freud and later interpreters, including need, demand and desire; fantasy; object a; love; anxiety; repetition; jouissance; sexual non-relation; transference and intimate relationships.

Extended clinical and interpretive sources

These additional files deepen the clinical and relationship-focused material. They should supplement rather than replace the canonical M01–M10 structure.

  1. Bruce Fink — A Clinical Introduction to Lacanian Psychoanalysis Clinical orientation to Lacanian psychoanalysis, including analytic listening, diagnosis, desire, interpretation, transference and technique.
  2. Diego Busiol — Lacanian Psychoanalysis in Practice Practice-focused perspectives from Lacanian analysts, including analytic listening, intervention, desire, enjoyment and contemporary clinical presentations.
  3. Mary Morgan — Couple Relations: A Contemporary Introduction Psychoanalytic account of couple relations, including partner choice, projective systems, shared unconscious phantasy, otherness, the couple as a third, sexuality, love and hate.
  4. Clinical Handbook of Couple Therapy Broader clinical reference for couple-therapy models and applications, useful for comparison between Lacanian or psychoanalytic formulations and other approaches to couple work.

Quick links

  • Companions and Clinical Perspectives — best starting point for clinical application.
